# CS 255 – System Analysis and Design

## DriverPass System Design Project

This repository contains my Project One Business Requirements Document and Project Two System Design Document, developed for CS 255 at Southern New Hampshire University. Together, these documents demonstrate my understanding of requirements gathering, systems analysis, and object-oriented system design.

## Project Overview

DriverPass was a client seeking a web-based system to help students prepare for their driving examinations through online practice tests and behind-the-wheel training. The proposed system needed to support customer registration, package purchases, lesson scheduling, practice testing, and administrative operations. The project involved translating the client's business requirements into a structured system design that addressed both functionality and usability.

## Strengths and Accomplishments

One aspect of my work that I believe I handled particularly well was translating the client's requirements into clearly defined system processes. I focused on ensuring that the UML diagrams reflected the actual business rules rather than simply illustrating individual functions. For example, the lesson scheduling process verifies that customers have sufficient package hours and that a driver and vehicle are available before confirming a reservation. This helped establish a connection between the client's expectations and the proposed system's behavior.

## Areas for Improvement

If I could revise one aspect of the project, I would improve the level of detail in the UML sequence diagrams, particularly how the system handles unsuccessful transactions or unexpected conditions. Although the diagrams represent the primary workflows, additional alternative scenarios could better illustrate how the system responds to database errors, scheduling conflicts, or failed operations. Including these details would improve the completeness of the design and provide developers with clearer guidance during implementation.

## Understanding and Implementing User Needs

I interpreted the client's needs by examining the DriverPass interview transcript, identifying the responsibilities of different users, and organizing the requirements into functional and nonfunctional categories. I then used those requirements to develop UML diagrams that represented how customers, employees, and administrators would interact with the system.

Considering user needs is essential because a technically functional system may still be ineffective if it does not support the people who depend on it. Understanding user expectations early in the design process helps prevent unnecessary revisions, improves usability, and ensures that the completed system addresses the client's actual business objectives.

## My Approach to Software Design

My approach to software design begins with understanding the problem before selecting a technical solution. I prefer to identify stakeholders, examine existing workflows, document requirements, and evaluate possible limitations before moving into system modeling.

Throughout this course, I learned how different UML diagrams provide complementary perspectives of the same system. Use case diagrams identify interactions between users and the system, activity diagrams illustrate workflows and decisions, sequence diagrams describe interactions over time, and class diagrams represent the system's structural components.

In future projects, I plan to continue using iterative requirements analysis, UML modeling, stakeholder feedback, and systematic design reviews. I also intend to place greater emphasis on validating alternative workflows and ensuring that every major requirement can be traced to a corresponding element of the system design.
